Atelier / Clother

A mobile-first fashion commerce concept that turns an editorial point of view into a readable shopping path. I shaped the browsing rhythm so atmosphere supports, rather than competes with, the next decision.

Context, scope and role

Atelier is a self-initiated concept project inspired by the tension between editorial fashion storytelling and the practical rhythm of online shopping. The live experience establishes a Fall / Winter collection, New In products, curated collections and an editorial story layer.

My contribution

UI/UX direction, responsive interface design, visual hierarchy and front-end implementation.

Design scope

Home, discovery, collection storytelling, product entry points and editorial content rhythm.

Primary constraint

Maintain a distinctive fashion mood without making product discovery feel like a poster rather than an interface.

Success criteria

Users should understand the point of view, find a product route and recognize the next action without extra explanation.

The design challenge

How might a fashion shopping experience feel editorial and expressive while still making discovery, comparison and purchase feel effortless? The design answer is not to remove the mood; it is to give every expressive element a job in the path.

From signal to decision

01 / Editorial entry

The hero establishes the collection point of view first, then exposes a direct route into shopping instead of asking the image to carry the whole experience.

02 / Product rhythm

New In turns the broad mood into scannable product moments: Tailored Coat, Silk Gown and Urban Knit each receive a clear visual and seasonal cue.

03 / Collection context

Featured Collections and The Edit extend the story without mixing editorial reading with product-level decisions too early.

04 / Action clarity

Shop Now and Read Story remain explicit, so users can choose between a transactional path and a deeper brand path.

What the design enables

The current build gives the brand a clear sequence: establish the world, introduce new products, offer a collection route and then open a longer editorial read. It is a qualitative design outcome, not a measured conversion result.

What remains unverified: no analytics, usability-test recordings or purchase data were supplied for this concept. The next validation would test whether users can find a product, distinguish product content from editorial content and complete the intended next action on mobile.

Reflection and next iteration

Atelier shows how visual restraint can protect an expressive concept. My next iteration would focus on the product detail and checkout states, because that is where editorial confidence must become practical confidence: size, material, availability and return information should be as considered as the opening image.
