B2B WEB DESIGN · INFORMATION HIERARCHY

CENNEXT

A constrained web-design test where the design job was to make dense industrial content feel actionable without rewriting the brief.

01 / CONTEXT

A design test with hard content constraints

This project was created from a supplied industrial electric motor-repair brief. The content requirement was unusually strict: the provided H1/H2/H3 wording and body copy had to remain intact, all major sections had to live on one page, and the visual direction had to retain a navy / amber / pale-blue industrial language. My responsibility was to create hierarchy, pacing and reusable responsive sections without solving the problem by shortening the content.

ConstraintExact supplied contentDesign around copy, not instead of it.
Primary userIndustrial decision-makerNeeds confidence, capability and next action.
Primary pathDiagnose → evaluate → trust → contactSeven sections, one decision journey.
Evidence statusPrototype + brief complianceNo lead or conversion data claimed.
02 / DESIGN PROBLEM

When copy cannot shrink, hierarchy has to work harder

The risk was a long technical page that felt like a wall of maintenance information. I treated each section as a distinct question in the buyer's decision: what fails, what can be repaired, whether recurring support exists, when to act, whether repair or replacement makes sense, why this provider is credible, and how to request help.

01 / OrientSticky wayfinding

A primary header plus seven-item section navigation keeps the long page legible without splitting the brief into separate pages.

02 / ExplainChunk technical evidence

Lists, capability cards, callouts and photography turn dense prose into scan paths while preserving the required copy.

03 / CompareSupport a real decision

The repair-vs-replace content becomes a comparison table and decision frame rather than another paragraph block.

04 / ActRepeat the right CTA

Contact Our Repair Team appears where intent is strongest, not as decorative button repetition.

03 / SYSTEM & HANDOFF

Reusable sections mattered more than a one-off hero

B2B EVIDENCE LAYER

Section grammar

Labels, headings, text modules, callouts, cards and comparison states create a repeatable structure across a long-form page.

Brand translation

Condensed display typography, utility mono labels and navy/amber accents make the interface feel industrial without resorting to generic machinery-dashboard styling.

Implementation readiness

The final version was converted to semantic HTML/CSS/JS with no framework or build dependency, making the handoff easy to inspect and host.

04 / PRODUCT REASONING

The hierarchy makes a business bet: reduce uncertainty before asking for contact

The project goal is not merely to make technical copy readable. The interface needs to help a potential buyer decide whether the provider appears capable enough to contact. That creates a trade-off between fast conversion and enough technical evidence to support a high-consideration B2B decision.

User valueUnderstand capability without decoding a wall of copy

The structure should help an operations or maintenance reader locate failure symptoms, repair scope, comparison criteria and evidence with less scanning effort.

Owner valueGenerate a more qualified contact

The page should move an informed prospect toward repair-team contact after capability and fit are understandable—not simply maximize button exposure.

Trade-offEvidence depth vs. speed

More technical proof can increase confidence but also lengthen the page. Sticky section navigation and repeated contextual CTAs preserve direct access without deleting required content.

Success signalComprehension before click-through

A user should be able to explain the relevant repair capability, compare repair versus replacement and identify the next contact action without assistance.

ALTERNATIVE CONSIDERED

Splitting the brief into multiple service pages would reduce single-page density, but it would violate the supplied one-page test constraint and introduce extra navigation cost. The chosen direction therefore optimizes scan structure inside the mandated format rather than pretending the constraint does not exist.

05 / VALIDATION PLAN

What should be tested before calling the page effective

Current state: PLANNED VALIDATION. Brief compliance and implementation are real evidence; B2B comprehension and lead quality are not measured outcomes. A production follow-up should validate the decision path with people who understand or purchase industrial maintenance services.

Task 01Find the relevant repair capability

Give a plausible motor-failure scenario and observe whether the user can locate the relevant service/capability and explain why it fits.

Task 02Compare repair versus replacement

Ask the user to use the page to explain when repair may make sense and what information is still needed before a real decision.

Task 03Reach the correct next action

Ask the user to request help after evaluation. Observe whether CTA wording, placement and contact expectations are clear.

Evidence to captureErrors, recovery and confidence

Track task completion, wrong section jumps, repeated scanning, comparison comprehension, CTA discovery and the questions users still need answered.

REPAIR RULE

If users repeatedly depend on sticky navigation because section order does not match their mental model, the section sequence should be reconsidered. If they reach contact without understanding scope, the CTA may be too early even if click-through appears high.

06 / OUTCOME & BOUNDARY

What the submission proves

The finished page preserves the supplied content, required sections and visual constraints while turning them into a navigable B2B decision path. It demonstrates content hierarchy, brand translation, responsive composition and design-to-code handoff under constraints.

NOT CLAIMED

This was a design-test prototype, not a live commercial launch. No CRM data, lead-quality data or conversion analytics were provided. The validation plan above defines what would need to be observed before claiming that the structure improves buyer comprehension or business performance.

07 / REFLECTION

Constraint exposed the difference between layout and information design

Because I could not solve density by deleting content, every visual decision had to improve comprehension. The project reinforced a useful rule for B2B work: hierarchy should reveal the buyer's questions in order, while the brand system makes those answers feel coherent and credible. The next iteration would focus on mobile scan length, comparison-table behavior and evidence placement around the contact decision, then revise the structure from observed task behavior rather than visual preference.
