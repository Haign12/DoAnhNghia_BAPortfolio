Problem & role

Keep financial control visible as money moves.

The project brief centres on finance teams managing international payments. A disconnected approval chain makes the next approver, policy trigger and result harder to explain.

I owned product design and the working prototype: translating the brief into information architecture, approval workflows, permission rules and explicit operational states.

Design decisions

Make the control chain understandable.

  1. Keep the payment and its approvals together

    Connect initiation, policy evaluation, sequential review and execution to one record so context does not disappear between steps.

  2. Prefer readable rules over a visual node editor

    Use explicit IF/THEN policies and table-first density to support comparison and audit work without unnecessary interaction complexity.

  3. Design beyond the happy path

    Treat rejection, expiry, failure and permission denial as first-class states, alongside role-specific actions and a retained activity trail.

Outcome & limits

What this work demonstrates.

The working concept makes the approval model, operational hierarchy and recovery states inspectable. It demonstrates product and systems reasoning without claiming production banking impact.

Evidence boundary

Independent concept. Finance roles and workflows come from the project context, not interviews. Practitioner validation, customer adoption and operational impact remain unmeasured.

The next test should ask finance operators to explain a blocked payment, make an approval decision and predict the effect of a policy change.
