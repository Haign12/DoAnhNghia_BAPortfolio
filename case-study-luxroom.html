Problem & role

Help shoppers judge fit before committing.

Furniture decisions need more than an attractive image. Dimensions, material, availability and delivery all matter, but exposing everything at once can overwhelm discovery.

I owned UI/UX direction, content hierarchy, responsive design and the front-end prototype, balancing a calm visual experience with practical purchase information.

Design decisions

Move from inspiration to a clear choice.

  1. Start with the room

    Use a familiar space as the entry point, then connect that context to relevant products.

  2. Reveal detail with intent

    Introduce material and visual cues early; make dimensions, finish, stock and delivery prominent during evaluation.

  3. Keep the choice recoverable

    Carry product configuration into cart and checkout, with a way to revisit choices instead of restarting.

INTERFACE PROOF

Premium is carried by hierarchy, not by hiding information

Progressive information density

Material and visual cues appear early; dimensions, finish, stock and delivery become prominent closer to purchase.

Commerce continuity

Variant, delivery and location context are carried into cart and checkout instead of becoming detached metadata.

Outcome & limits

What this work demonstrates.

The implemented prototype demonstrates responsive hierarchy and continuity from discovery to configuration and checkout. It shows my design and implementation work, not measured commercial performance.

Evidence boundary

Independent concept. No observed commerce testing, purchase analytics or conversion impact is available. The next validation should test product fit, configuration confidence and checkout recovery.

The design lesson: a premium interface can stay calm while making practical decisions explicit.
