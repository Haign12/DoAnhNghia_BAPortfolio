FURNITURE · PRODUCT DECISION UX

LuxRoom

A furniture experience organized around space, material and practical confidence instead of a dense catalog-first journey.

01 / CONTEXT

From inspiration to an actual product decision

LuxRoom is a self-initiated, implemented furniture-commerce prototype. I owned UI/UX direction, responsive design, content hierarchy and front-end implementation. The design question was how to keep the calm, premium character expected from interiors while still exposing the specifications and logistics people need before buying furniture.

02 / DECISION MODEL

The journey moves from context to specification

01 / ContextChoose a room

Living space becomes a more intuitive entry point than an abstract product taxonomy.

02 / MaterialBuild preference

Surface and atmosphere establish relevance before technical density increases.

03 / ProductCheck fit

Dimensions, finish, availability and lead time support a higher-consideration decision.

04 / CommitSave or checkout

Saved Room and checkout let users preserve context rather than restart their decision.

03 / INTERFACE PROOF

Premium is carried by hierarchy, not by hiding information

FINAL IMPLEMENTED UI

Room-oriented discovery

Space context gives users a concrete way to enter the catalog without losing the visual story.

Progressive information density

Material and visual cues appear early; dimensions, finish, stock and delivery become prominent closer to purchase.

Commerce continuity

Variant, delivery and location context are carried into cart and checkout instead of becoming detached metadata.

04 / OUTCOME & BOUNDARY

What the prototype proves — and what it does not

The implemented concept demonstrates a complete interaction direction from contextual discovery to a multi-step purchase path, including richer product information and saved-room behavior. It does not demonstrate business performance.

NOT CLAIMED

No product analytics, moderated usability findings or purchase data were generated. The next validation should test room-to-product findability, comprehension of dimensions/material/delivery information and whether the contact/support path feels helpful rather than interruptive.

05 / REFLECTION

Calm interfaces can still carry dense decisions

The strongest direction was not reducing information; it was sequencing it. Furniture needs more practical evidence than an editorial gallery can provide. The next iteration should focus on comparison and delivery confidence, especially on smaller screens where dimensions and spatial fit become harder to interpret.
