Office Group Order Optimization

Modeling the As-Is vs To-Be process of office group ordering to eliminate payment bottlenecks.

1 WeekTimeline

📌 Personal / Simulated Project: This is a personal initiative tested with 5 real group food orders at the office. Impact data is based on manual timing (phone stopwatch) across these 5 trials. Sample size is limited and results should be interpreted as directional, not statistically significant.

1. The Problem

At the office, the daily process of ordering group lunches or milk tea is a logistical nightmare. The process flows through a group chat where people constantly change their minds, forget to pay, and force the Admin to manually reconcile orders and shipping fees.

This manual coordination results in a high "Payment Deficit" (Admin losing money) and a severe waste of productive work hours.

2. Constraints & Trade-offs

The Constraint: Third-party food delivery apps (like ShopeeFood or Grab) have group order features, but they often lack flexible payment splitting and don't allow the Admin to collect funds before placing the order.

The Trade-off: Instead of relying on rigid third-party apps, I modeled a custom To-Be process using a simple internal form linked to a QR payment gateway. The trade-off was a slight learning curve for staff, but absolute financial security for the Admin.

Constraints & Assumptions Log

⚠️ C-01: URL Scraper — Terms of Service Risk

The "Paste ShopeeFood/GrabFood link" feature relies on web scraping to extract menu data. This approach may violate the Terms of Service (ToS) of these platforms, which typically prohibit automated data extraction.

Mitigation: For production deployment, this feature should be replaced by official Merchant API partnerships (GrabFood for Business API) or manual menu upload. The scraper is acceptable only for internal prototype/pilot purposes.

⚠️ C-02: Internal Wallet — Financial Compliance Risk

The Internal Wallet feature holds user funds as credits rather than refunding to bank accounts. This raises critical compliance questions:

  • Who bears liability for wallet balances if the operating company ceases operations?
  • Is the wallet considered an e-money instrument requiring a license under Vietnamese law (Decree 101/2012)?
  • Audit trail requirements: all wallet transactions must be traceable and exportable.

Mitigation: For enterprise deployment, engage legal counsel to determine if an e-money license is required. Implement mandatory wallet-to-bank withdrawal option. Add expiration policy for dormant credits (≥12 months).

3. Approach & BA Artifacts

A. Process Modeling (As-Is)
I mapped the current state using BPMN 2.0. The diagram clearly highlighted the "Payment Collection" node as the critical bottleneck. Admin was advancing cash and acting as a debt collector post-delivery.

B. Bottleneck Detection
By timing the process, I found the Admin spent an average of 45 minutes per order just tracing chat messages to match drinks with faces, and calculating the divided shipping fee.

C. Process Optimization (To-Be)
I engineered a new flow: System calculates exact split -> Staff pays via QR -> System verifies 100% funds -> Order placed. No cash advanced, zero risk.

4. Core System Functions & UI Design

Beyond the interface, I defined strict functional requirements for the MVP:

  • Group Ordering Host: An initiator creates a session, and the system generates a sharable joining link for team members.
  • Auto-Consolidation & Split: The system aggregates item choices, calculates subtotal, applies discounts, and fairly divides the shipping fee.
  • Dynamic QR Payment: Automatically generates a bank QR code embedding the exact split amount and a predefined transfer syntax (e.g., [User]_Pay_OrderID).

I utilized process visualization to pitch the new workflow to the team, demonstrating the exact elimination of the bottleneck via a Neo-Brutalism interface design.

Real-world UI/UX Benchmark

An internal office tool demands raw efficiency over form. I chose Neo-Brutalism and Retro aesthetics to emphasize stark functionality and hyper-contrast:

  • Gumroad: The "textbook" for Neo-Brutalism. Inspired the aggressive 2px solid black borders, hard offset shadows, and high-contrast color palette (like Lavender Rose) to make actions unmistakable.
  • PostHog: Inspired the retro, highly functional aesthetic that makes technical/administrative tasks feel less dry and more engaging.
  • Yale School of Art: Inspired the raw, asymmetrical layouts and straightforward typography that prioritize truth and utility.

C. Software Requirements Specification (SRS) Excerpt

Following IEEE 830 / ISO 29148 standards, I drafted a structured SRS highlighting strict Business Rules for financial accuracy.

3.1.2 Business Rules (BR)

  • BR-01 (Order Cutoff): The system SHALL automatically lock the ordering session at the Host-defined cutoff time. Late entries SHALL be rejected to prevent shipping fee recalculation.
  • BR-02 (Fee Distribution): The total shipping fee and external discounts SHALL be divided equally among all unique participating users, regardless of individual order volume.
  • BR-03 (Payment Verification): The Host SHALL NOT be allowed to trigger the "Place Order to Vendor" action until the system registers 100% of required funds transferred via the Dynamic QR gateway.
  • BR-04 (Auto-cancellation): If past cutoff and a participant hasn't paid, the system SHALL auto-cancel their items and recalculate shipping split for remaining paid members.

6. Handling Edge Cases & Exception Flows

A "Happy Path" is never enough for a robust enterprise tool. To ensure the system handles real-world friction, I designed logic for the following edge cases:

A. The "Unpaid Participant" Scenario (Countdown Timer)

The Problem: What happens if the group orders, but 1 person forgets to pay via QR before the 2:30 PM deadline?

The Solution (Business Rule): I introduced a Countdown Timer. If the deadline expires and a participant is marked Unpaid, the system triggers an auto-cancellation of their specific items. It then instantly recalculates the shipping split for the remaining Paid members. A Push Notification / Slack ping is automatically sent via the Participant List reminder feature.

B. The "Out of Stock" Scenario (Internal Wallet Refund)

The Problem: The system collected 100% of the funds and placed the order. 5 minutes later, the vendor calls to cancel one item (e.g., "Out of Matcha"). Processing a direct bank refund via VNPay/Momo for 45,000đ is technically complex and incurs transaction fees.

The Solution (System Design): I proposed an Internal Wallet system. Instead of refunding to the bank, the 45,000đ is credited to the user's OrderFlow Wallet balance. The next time they join a group order, this balance is automatically deducted. This guarantees a seamless UX and eliminates banking transaction overhead.

C. Session Creation (Data Sourcing)

The Problem: Manually typing out menu items is tedious for the Admin.

The Solution (UX Flow): The Create Session screen features a URL scraper. The Admin simply pastes a ShopeeFood or GrabFood link, and the system automatically crawls the menu, prices, and options. Staff members receive a clean interface to "Add to Cart" with specific notes (e.g., "Less ice").

7. Business Impact & Results

"Applied the To-Be process in 5 real group food orders at the office. Timing was measured manually using a phone stopwatch. Admin coordination time dropped from an average of ~45 minutes to ~5 minutes per order. Zero instances of admin being short-changed across all 5 trials. Note: n=5 is insufficient for statistical significance but demonstrates a clear directional improvement."

  • ~80% reduction in coordination time across 5 real office trials (manually timed, n=5).
  • 0% payment deficit across all 5 trials (vs. frequent occurrences in the As-Is process).
  • Limitation: Small sample (n=5 orders, single office). Results are directional; enterprise-scale validation would require A/B testing across multiple departments.

Key Takeaway: Business Process Modeling Notation (BPMN) isn't just for factory floors. Applying it to everyday office logistics proves that a strong BA can detect inefficiencies and architect logical solutions anywhere.

8. Enterprise Scale Considerations

A critical exercise for any BA: what changes if this system scales from 1 office (10 people) to an enterprise with 50 branches and 5,000 employees?

A. Stakeholder Conflict Map

StakeholderPrimary GoalPotential Conflict
Admin (Order Coordinator)Speed — wants to finalize orders quicklyOpposes strict payment verification gates that slow down the process
Staff (End User)Convenience — wants minimal steps to orderResists mandatory pre-payment; prefers "pay later" flexibility
Finance DepartmentControl — wants audit trail & budget complianceDemands corporate wallet integration, spending limits per employee, and monthly reports
Vendor (Restaurant)Accuracy — wants precise, confirmed ordersRequires API integration for order accuracy; rejects phone/chat-based orders at scale
IT/SecurityCompliance — data privacy, payment securityBlocks any scraping features; demands PCI-DSS for payment processing

B. Architecture Changes at Scale

  • Multi-tenant Architecture: Each branch operates as a separate tenant with isolated data, but corporate Finance has a cross-tenant dashboard for consolidated reporting.
  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Branch Admin can only manage their own sessions. Regional Manager sees aggregate data. Finance has read-only audit access.
  • Vendor API Integration: Replace URL scraper with official GrabFood for Business / ShopeeFood Merchant APIs. Orders flow directly into vendor POS systems.
  • Concurrency Handling: Redis-based session locking to prevent race conditions when 50+ employees submit orders simultaneously at cutoff time.
  • Audit Trail & Compliance: Every transaction (order, payment, refund) logged with immutable audit trail. Monthly export to ERP system (SAP/Oracle) via scheduled ETL jobs.
