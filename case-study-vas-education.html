EDUCATION · INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE · PARENT JOURNEY

VAS Education

A school website redesign that shifts the organizing logic from “what the institution has to say” to “what a family needs to decide next.”

01 / CONTEXT

The content was not missing — the path through it was

VAS Education is an independent redesign concept for a school website with a broad information ecosystem: learning stages, programs, campuses, student life, outcomes, stories and admissions. I worked across product framing, information architecture, UX writing direction, UI/UX design, responsive interaction and front-end implementation.

Primary audienceProspective familiesHigh-consideration school choice.
Core problemInformation without a pathUseful content can still be hard to navigate.
Design modelQuestion-led journeyOrganize around what families need next.
Evidence statusPrototype + audit inputsNo parent research outcome claimed.
02 / AUDIENCE QUESTIONS

Different questions become important at different moments

01 / First impressionCan I trust this school?

Brand promise and credible proof need to arrive before curriculum complexity.

02 / FitWhich path fits my child?

Learning stages and pathways must be understandable as a connected journey.

03 / RealityWhat is daily life like?

Campus, teachers, student experience and parent voices make the promise concrete.

04 / ActionWhat do I do next?

Admissions, visits and questions need a calm next step rather than an abrupt form-first ending.

03 / ARCHITECTURE SHIFT

From content inventory to a guided decision sequence

The homepage was structured as a progressive argument: establish the promise, substantiate it, help families orient within the learning journey, show everyday experience and only then ask for action. This is a sequencing decision, not simply a new visual layout.

PromiseWhat VAS stands forSet meaning before detail.
ProofWhy believe itBring credible signals forward.
JourneyWhere does my child fitStage and pathway orientation.
Experience → ActionWhat happens nextDaily reality followed by admissions.
04 / PRODUCT HYPOTHESES

The redesign is a set of testable bets, not a finished truth

The strongest product decision is the question-led sequence, but the available evidence does not prove that it is the best sequence for real parents. I therefore treat the following as explicit hypotheses and attach a success signal to each one.

H1 / OrientationQuestion-led sequencing reduces early uncertainty

Expected signal: a parent can explain the school proposition and identify the next useful section without being told where to click.

H2 / FitConnected pathways improve program comprehension

Expected signal: a parent can identify a plausible learning pathway for a child and explain the difference between adjacent options.

H3 / ComparisonCampus context should support—not interrupt—the program decision

Expected signal: a parent can compare relevant campus options after choosing a pathway without losing their decision context.

H4 / ActionAdmissions CTA works better after sufficient reassurance

Expected signal: the user can reach a visit/enquiry action after exploration and describe what will happen next.

PRODUCT TRADE-OFF

Moving proof and orientation ahead of detailed program content may improve first-visit comprehension, but it can also add scrolling for families arriving with a highly specific program intent. A production design should therefore preserve direct navigation/search routes instead of forcing every user through the editorial sequence.

05 / VALIDATION PLAN

What I would test before claiming the information model works

Current state: PLANNED VALIDATION. No parent usability sessions are being presented as completed. A useful next round would recruit prospective or recently enrolled parents and test realistic school-choice tasks on the responsive prototype.

Task 01Find an appropriate pathway

Scenario: choose a plausible learning path for a child at a specified age/stage. Observe route choice, comprehension, backtracking and confidence.

Task 02Compare campus options

Scenario: after selecting a pathway, identify two relevant campuses and explain what matters for the decision. Watch for lost context or unclear labels.

Task 03Move from evaluation to visit

Scenario: explore until ready to learn more, then book or begin a school-visit/enquiry path. Check CTA discovery and expectation clarity.

Evidence to captureBehavior before opinion

Task completion, wrong turns, recovery, comprehension, confidence and recurring language problems. Iteration should be tied to observed issues rather than preference voting.

PASS CONDITION

The next iteration should not claim improvement until the test produces observable evidence. If parents consistently miss pathways, misunderstand campus relationships or hesitate at the admissions transition, the IA—not only the styling—must be revised.

06 / INTERFACE PROOF

The interface keeps warmth, but uses structure to carry complexity

RESPONSIVE INTERFACE MODEL

Trust before complexity

Editorial entry and proof precede program detail to reduce the cognitive burden of the first visit.

Journey over catalogue

Learning stages are presented as a relationship rather than isolated pages that families must assemble mentally.

Progressive disclosure

Campus, pathways, stories and admissions become deeper only when the context makes them useful.

07 / OUTCOME & BOUNDARY

A clearer prototype for a complex education decision

The implemented experience connects brand, proof, program choice, campus context and admissions in one continuous journey. It demonstrates a stronger information model and responsive interface direction, not measured admissions performance.

NOT CLAIMED

No parent interviews, moderated usability recordings, enquiry data or conversion metrics were produced for this concept. The product hypotheses and tasks above make the next evidence step explicit instead of presenting the prototype as validation.

08 / REFLECTION

Inspiration and decision support work better when they are sequenced

Education websites need both emotion and practical orientation. The redesign became clearer when those modes stopped competing inside the same block. The next iteration should use parent evidence to challenge the sequence itself, then make program/campus comparison more explicit where observed behavior shows it is needed.
