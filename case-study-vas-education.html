Context, scope and role

VAS Education is a redesign concept for a school website where families need to move from brand discovery to a confident admissions decision. The experience communicates VAS's international education model, learning journey, campuses, student life and admissions process across one connected system.

My contribution

Product framing, information architecture, UX writing direction, UI/UX design, responsive interaction design and front-end implementation.

Design scope

Homepage, education journey, programs, campus discovery, student life, stories, outcomes, parent voices and admissions.

Primary constraint

Make a large education system feel personal and navigable without reducing important proof, context or choice.

Success criteria

A parent should understand what VAS offers, find the information relevant to their child's stage and recognise a clear next action.

The design challenge

Choosing a school is not a quick browse. Parents are balancing trust, curriculum, age stage, location, daily experience and the practical question of what happens next. A conventional school website can present every fact but still leave the visitor unsure where to begin.

For this redesign, the core problem was therefore not a lack of content. It was the distance between a family's question and the information that could answer it. The interface needed to establish confidence first, then progressively reveal the system behind the promise.

Design thesis: turn a complex school ecosystem into a sequence of understandable decisions — discover the promise, understand the journey, compare the fit and take the next step.

Audience needs and decision moments

The experience is shaped around parents and families at different moments of consideration. Instead of assuming every visitor arrives with the same question, the structure makes several entry points visible without fragmenting the brand story.

01 / First impression

“What kind of school is this, and can I trust it with my child?” The hero and proof sections answer the emotional and credibility questions first.

02 / Fit and pathway

“Which learning journey and program could fit my child?” The education section turns stages and Cambridge pathways into a navigable story.

03 / Everyday reality

“What will daily life actually feel like?” Student learning, teachers, campus spaces and parent voices make the promise tangible.

04 / Next action

“How do I move forward without pressure?” The admissions section makes visiting and asking questions feel like a clear, low-friction next step.

From content inventory to a guided journey

The page is organised as a progressive narrative rather than a flat collection of menu links. Each section answers a natural follow-up question and prepares the user for the next one.

01 / Promise

The hero frames VAS as a long-term journey and pairs the primary message with two actions: book a visit or explore the school.

02 / Proof

Key numbers and outcome signals appear early, giving the narrative a credible foundation before the visitor enters deeper content.

03 / Journey

Learning stages and the three Cambridge pathways are grouped into an explorable model so families can orient themselves by age and ambition.

04 / Experience

Learning, people, campus, student stories and parent voices show how the promise becomes a lived experience.

05 / Action

Admissions closes the loop with a calm sequence from first question to visit, replacing an intimidating form-first moment.

06 / Navigation

Desktop mega navigation and a mobile menu preserve access to deep content while keeping the landing page narrative intact.

Key design decisions

Trust before complexity

The visual language starts with a confident editorial hero, proof points and human imagery before introducing program detail. This lowers the cognitive load of the first visit.

Journey over catalogue

Education stages are presented as a continuous path. The user can understand how one stage relates to the next instead of reading isolated program descriptions.

Progressive disclosure

Campus lists, pathway tabs, story cards and parent voices allow depth to appear when it becomes relevant, rather than placing every detail in the first viewport.

Warmth with structure

A restrained red, paper and gold palette creates a recognisable education brand while grid lines, labels and strong hierarchy keep the page practical to scan.

The interaction and visual system

The redesign uses a shared content and interaction language across sections. The system is designed to support both emotional storytelling and high-information moments such as pathway comparison, campus selection and admissions steps.

  • Persistent navigation gives the visitor access to education, campuses, stories and admissions from every scroll position.
  • Section-level eyebrow labels and numbered content groups make a long page easier to scan and remember.
  • Red surfaces carry high-intent moments while paper-toned surfaces give room to read, compare and reflect.
  • Responsive layouts change from multi-column storytelling to stacked content without hiding the primary CTA.
  • Reveal transitions add rhythm to the narrative while the core content remains available without relying on motion.

What the redesign enables

The implemented experience gives VAS a clearer digital front door. A first-time visitor can understand the promise, explore the learning journey, inspect the everyday experience and reach admissions without having to reconstruct the school's information architecture alone.

The strongest outcome is structural: the website connects brand, proof, program choice and action into one continuous experience. It makes “learn more” feel like a guided path rather than an instruction to search.

What remains unverified: no parent interviews, usability-test recordings, enquiry data or conversion metrics were supplied. The next validation should test three tasks with families: find a suitable pathway, compare two campuses and book a visit after exploring the homepage.

Reflection and next iteration

VAS Education reinforced that an education website must hold two modes at once: it has to inspire a family emotionally and help them make a practical decision. The redesign became clearer when those modes were sequenced instead of competing in the same block.

The next iteration would focus on evidence-led refinement: validate the information architecture with parents, improve program comparison for different age stages, connect campus content to location-based intent and measure which admissions paths create the most confident next step.
