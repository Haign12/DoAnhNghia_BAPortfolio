FRAGRANCE MARKETPLACE · DISCOVERY UX

Violet Marketplace

A luxury fragrance marketplace that treats discovery as evaluation, not endless browsing.

01 / CONTEXT

What I designed — and what this project is

Violet is a self-initiated fragrance marketplace prototype built in HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript. I designed the buyer-facing discovery model, product hierarchy, visual system and commerce flow while preserving separate seller and admin surfaces. Product data, maisons, cart state and checkout are prototype data; the project does not claim real inventory, fulfilment or commercial partnerships.

Core problemScent is hard to evaluate onlineA bottle image and price are not enough.
Primary loopExplore → understand → try → returnDiscovery before commitment.
System choiceImage-first 70 / 30Media leads; buying information stays legible.
Evidence statusPrototype verifiedNo conversion outcome claimed.
02 / DESIGN PROBLEM

A perfume marketplace cannot behave like a generic product catalog

Fragrance is bought through language, memory, material and risk tolerance as much as specification. A generic category grid collapses that decision into brand, bottle and price. I reframed the experience around olfactive family, mood, notes, maison and try-first eligibility, so users can enter through the vocabulary they actually have.

01 / EnterChoose a discovery language

Browse by family, mood, notes, house or a guided Scent Portrait.

02 / UnderstandMake scent more tangible

Use object, material and atmosphere imagery alongside note structure and descriptive copy.

03 / Reduce riskOffer a try-first path

Discovery trio logic separates curiosity from full-bottle commitment.

04 / CompleteReturn to familiar commerce

Bag and checkout become calmer and more conventional once intent is high.

03 / SYSTEM & INTERACTION

The visual signature supports the product model instead of decorating it

MATERIAL + ATMOSPHERE LAYER

Violet Index

Restrained labels such as V.01 and HOUSE 03 create wayfinding without turning the interface into dashboard chrome.

Progressive density

Homepage and PDP stay image-led; operational surfaces such as bag, checkout, seller and admin become denser and calmer.

Truthful prototype boundaries

The interface explicitly labels fictional maisons, browser-local behavior and the absence of real AI, payment or fulfilment.

04 / OUTCOME & BOUNDARY

What the prototype demonstrates

The implemented flow demonstrates that a marketplace can combine editorial luxury with decision support: users can browse a fragrance library, open product detail, build a discovery trio, use a guided Scent Portrait, add to bag and move through checkout without the brand layer obscuring basic commerce controls.

NOT CLAIMED

No live customer research, conversion data, fulfilment operation or sales analytics were produced. The next validation should test whether users understand the difference between full-bottle purchase and try-first discovery, whether Scent Portrait feels useful without overpromising intelligence, and whether note/mood filters improve findability.

05 / REFLECTION

Luxury UX is mostly the discipline of removing noise without removing evidence

The strongest design decision was not the violet palette or editorial serif. It was separating showroom behavior from operational behavior. Discovery benefits from atmosphere and generous media; checkout benefits from familiarity. The next iteration should add responsive validation and moderated task testing around fragrance findability, trial selection and product-confidence questions.
