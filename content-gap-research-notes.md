# UI/UX case study content-gap research

## Scope and assumptions

Target: the UI/UX work section of DoAnhNghia_BAPortfolio, with three portfolio projects: Atelier, LuxRoom and StudioOS. The current pages are visually coherent but thin in decision narrative: they describe the design direction, yet they do not consistently show project context, problem framing, method, iterations, outcome, limitations or next steps. No private analytics, usability-test results, client metrics or research transcripts were supplied. The case studies must therefore distinguish observed facts from design rationale and must not invent business impact.

## Public benchmark findings

The benchmark sources consistently describe a UX case study as a linear story that explains the problem, the design process, the decisions made and the impact or learning, rather than a gallery of screens.

1. UXfolio, “The Ultimate UX Case Study Template & Structure (2026 Guide),” https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-case-study-template/
   - Recommends a concise structure built around hero, project overview, exploration/discovery, UX process, final design, impact and learnings.
   - Emphasizes explaining why decisions were made, adding contextualized visuals and being honest when metrics are unavailable.
   - States that a junior portfolio commonly benefits from 2–3 strong case studies and that quality matters more than quantity.

2. UXPin, “UX Case Study – Step-by-Step Guide [+Template],” https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/ux-case-study/
   - Frames the case study around introduction, process, research, design iterations and final observations/result.
   - Calls for explicit problem statements, information architecture, research methods, design decisions and testing/iteration evidence.
   - Recommends concise, scannable content for hiring managers.

3. Interaction Design Foundation, “How to Write Great Case Studies for Your UX Design Portfolio,” https://ixdf.org/literature/article/how-to-write-great-case-studies-for-your-ux-design-portfolio
   - Recommends a story structure: hook, research, sketches/wireframes, prototyping/iteration, outcomes and lessons learned.
   - Advises showing role, contribution, collaboration, constraints and an early result preview.
   - Notes that the outcome/lessons section is important even when a project has no formal business metric.

4. Coursera Staff, “UX Designer Portfolio: A Beginner’s Guide,” https://www.coursera.org/articles/ux-designer-portfolio-beginners-guide
   - Describes the portfolio core as home, about and case-study/work sections.
   - Lists context, scope, role, problem, method, research, user journey, iterations, final product, success criteria and lessons learned as useful case-study content.
   - Explicitly recommends acknowledging the scope and limitations of concept or self-initiated work.

## Gap-to-build mapping

| Existing gap | Case-study treatment |
|---|---|
| Project context and scope are implicit | Add a compact overview row with type, role, platform, scope and status. |
| Design question exists but user/problem framing is light | Add “The challenge” with the assumed user need and clearly label concept assumptions. |
| Current pages show principles but not evidence of progression | Add “From signal to decision” with a short sequence of observation, interpretation and design response. |
| Outcomes are mostly qualitative | Use “What the design enables” and “What remains unverified”; do not fabricate conversion or usability metrics. |
| No consistent end section across projects | Add reflection, limitations and next iteration for all three projects. |
| SEO pages are mostly title/description plus thin body copy | Add descriptive H2 sections, internal links between case studies and `CreativeWork`/`Article` JSON-LD where appropriate. |

## Writing rule

Use “I designed,” “the interface prioritizes,” and “this concept explores” for portfolio facts and design rationale. Use “would test,” “hypothesis,” “not measured” and “next validation” for anything not supported by real project evidence.
