Date: 2026-09-28
Portfolio: https://do-anh-nghia-uiux-portfolio.vercel.app/
Target direction: Product Designer → Product Design leadership / management track

## Evidence policy

This roadmap follows the same truth contract as UIUX Factory:

- **VERIFIED** — directly visible in repository, shipped prototype, case study, or documented professional scope.
- **INFERRED** — reasonable conclusion from visible evidence, but not directly measured.
- **PLANNED** — a concrete activity or measurement that has not happened yet.
- **UNKNOWN** — no reliable evidence available.

No participant count, metric uplift, mentoring relationship, management responsibility, analytics result, stakeholder approval, or business impact should be presented as real unless a source can be retained.

## Step 0 conclusion

**Tier A is promising but not yet complete.** The strongest evidence is problem framing, systems thinking, implementation discipline and trade-off articulation. The blocking gaps before a credible Product Design Manager claim are **analytics, business alignment, real cross-functional decision-making, opportunity sizing and post-launch learning**.

The correct near-term positioning is therefore:

> **Product Designer focused on decision-heavy financial/B2B systems, with a deliberate design-leadership track through DesignOps, governance and team enablement.**

Do **not** claim “Product Design Manager” as a current title until real people-management responsibility exists.

# Step 2 — Portfolio upgrade contract

## 2.1 About / Personal Story

### Short version

I started in UI/UX delivery, where I learned that polished screens are rarely the hardest part — the harder problem is turning ambiguous requirements into decisions a team can understand, build and evaluate. That pushed my work toward product framing, systems and evidence, and eventually led me to build UIUX Factory as a repeatable design-to-QA operating workflow. Today I focus on decision-heavy financial and B2B products while deliberately developing the people, process and business skills required for design leadership.

### Long version

I began in UI/UX delivery across agency and product work, initially measuring progress in flows, screens and implementation quality. The more I worked with responsive systems and developer handoff, the more I noticed that the difficult part was not drawing the interface. It was deciding what problem deserved attention, what evidence should change the direction, what the team needed to agree on, and what would count as a successful result.

That changed how I work. My strongest projects now start from a decision or operating problem — financial confidence in Nova, evidence-to-action in Sentry, or repeatable quality control in UIUX Factory — and make the assumptions, trade-offs, system rules and validation boundaries explicit. I am especially interested in fintech, B2B operational products and DesignOps because they force design to balance trust, information density, risk, implementation reality and measurable outcomes.

I am currently a Product Designer on a deliberate design-leadership track. I am not presenting management experience I have not earned yet. My next stage is to turn strong individual product practice into operating leverage: mentoring, critique facilitation, onboarding, design-system governance, stakeholder business cases and post-launch learning loops that make a team stronger rather than only making my own output better.

## 2.3 Thought Leadership article

**Title:** From AI tools to design governance: building a verifiable UI/UX operating system

**Opening**
- AI makes interface generation cheap; it does not make product judgment cheap.
- The failure mode is faster production of plausible but unverified design.
- UIUX Factory began as a response to that gap.

**Body 1 — The problem is not generation, it is decision ownership**
- Separate user/business/design/engineering/release authority.
- Keep VERIFIED / INFERRED / ASSUMED / UNKNOWN distinct.
- AI can prepare options and evidence; humans retain consequential authority.

**Body 2 — Turn design process into explicit contracts**
- Project truth before art direction.
- Decision ledger: evidence, alternative, trade-off, technical reality, success signal.
- ADOPT / ADAPT / REJECT prevents reference copying from masquerading as strategy.

**Body 3 — QA is part of design, not a final engineering ceremony**
- Rendered pixels, responsive pressure, accessibility, browser state, performance.
- Build green ≠ product quality.
- Root-cause repair sends failures back to the stage that owns them.

**Body 4 — What this changes for a team**
- Review criteria become teachable.
- Onboarding can point to a shared operating model.
- Design-system changes can have ownership and approval rules.
- Critique becomes evidence-led rather than taste-led.

**Conclusion**
- The leadership lesson is not “use more AI”.
- It is “make judgment, ownership and learning more explicit so the team can move faster without making truth optional.”

# Step 3 — Project upgrade method

Apply this to each flagship before calling the portfolio manager-ready.

## Business problem framing

For every project add:

1. User / operator problem.
2. Owner/business problem.
3. Baseline: VERIFIED value or UNKNOWN.
4. Opportunity size: user count / frequency / time / money / risk — VERIFIED or clearly ESTIMATED.
5. Product objective / OKR connection.
6. Feature role in roadmap: now / next / later and why.

## Measurement methods

Use the lightest credible method:

- **Nova:** usability test + proxy metrics + benchmark.
- **Sentry:** task-based operational usability + error/recovery metrics + simulated throughput.
- **UIUX Factory:** before/after process benchmark on the same project task — review defects, handoff ambiguity, QA failures, repair cycles, time-to-evidence.

## 1–2 day test template

- Participants: **5 minimum** for a directional moderated/unmoderated usability round when the target role can be recruited; otherwise label the limitation.
- Task success: completed / partial / failed.
- Time on task.
- Critical errors / recovery errors.
- Confidence rating 1–5.
- SUS only when the session covers enough of the system for the score to be meaningful.
- Record raw notes in Sheets; keep participant IDs anonymous; retain task definitions and timestamped results.

## Seven-part case-study structure

1. Title & summary.
2. Context & problem — user + business + metric target.
3. Role & responsibilities — actual team and decision ownership.
4. Discovery — research/data/insight.
5. Solution & decisions — alternatives, trade-offs, sequencing.
6. Results & impact — label VERIFIED / ESTIMATED / PLANNED.
7. Post-launch iteration & learning.

Mandatory inserts:

- Collaboration with PM / Eng / Data — only real roles.
- Product Strategy / roadmap placement.
- Product impact metric.
- Post-launch or planned learning loop.

# Step 4 — 6-month leadership roadmap

| Month | Product foundation | Leadership / mentoring | Process / org | Executive communication | Portfolio evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Run one real usability round on Nova or Sentry; define event schema and metric tree. | Review one peer portfolio and document feedback framework. | Write design critique template + decision log. | Create one 1-page business case for a flagship. | Publish recorded test method/results with evidence labels. |
| **2** | Build a mock Mixpanel/Amplitude-style funnel from defined events; practice segmentation and hypothesis reading. | Find one junior/intern/peer who explicitly wants recurring feedback. | Define UIUX Factory onboarding + contribution rules. | Present product objective → metric → design decision in 10 minutes to a mentor/peer. | Add Collaboration and Product Strategy sections to 2 flagships. |
| **3** | Add opportunity sizing and roadmap sequencing to 2 cases. | Run at least 2 critique/mentoring sessions with goals and follow-up. | Create design-system governance: owner / contributor / reviewer / approval. | Write a stakeholder memo comparing 2 options and recommendation with trade-offs. | Add Leadership Practice evidence only for completed activities. |
| **4** | Run a second validation iteration and compare with the same tasks. | Create a lightweight growth plan for the person you are helping, with their consent. | Pilot an onboarding/handbook artifact with another designer. | Practice handling disagreement: record decision, evidence, unresolved risk. | Publish UIUX Factory leadership case study. |
| **5** | Create one production-learning style case even if using a small real project: instrumentation → observation → iteration. | Facilitate a structured critique with 3+ participants if available. | Measure one process signal: review cycle time, defect count, missing states, or handoff questions. | Deliver a roadmap/storytelling presentation with data to a mentor or stakeholder. | Publish Thought Leadership article. |
| **6** | Consolidate metric literacy: conversion, retention, activation, CAC/LTV/MRR concepts relevant to target companies. | Ask for written feedback on leadership behaviors from collaborators. | Retrospect: which operating practice actually helped another person/team? | Create Manager-track interview stories using Situation → Decision → Influence → Outcome → Learning. | Final portfolio audit; prepare Product Designer/Senior-track CV and leadership-track narrative without title inflation. |

## What 6 months can and cannot achieve

**Can achieve:** much stronger Product Designer evidence, credible leadership behaviors, mentoring/critique/process proof, better business/data fluency, a differentiated DesignOps story.

**Cannot truthfully manufacture:** years of people management, hiring, performance reviews, org design, managing managers, or company-level design strategy. Those require real organizational responsibility over time.

# CV / LinkedIn guidance

Keep historical titles exactly as employed: **UI/UX Designer** / **Intern UI/UX Designer**.

Use headline/summary to express direction, e.g.:

> Product Designer · Fintech & B2B systems · Design systems & AI-assisted DesignOps · Building toward design leadership

## Leadership bullet formula

`Problem / scope → leadership behavior → collaborators → decision/process → verified result or evidence-safe output`

Examples that are safe only when true:

- “Created a reusable handoff checklist adopted across X projects, clarifying responsive states and reducing repeated implementation questions.”
- “Facilitated a weekly design critique for X designers, documenting decisions and follow-up owners.”
- “Mentored a junior designer for X weeks on hierarchy and component thinking; tracked goals and review notes.”

Focus on:

- Decision-heavy product design.

- Design-to-code and systems.
