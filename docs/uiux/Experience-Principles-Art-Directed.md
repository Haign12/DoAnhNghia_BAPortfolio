# Experience Principles — Art-Directed Portfolio

Date: 2026-09-04

## 1. Explore before explain
Lead with a strong authored visual statement and an interactive work index before long process explanation.

Design implications:
- Home immediately exposes role + selected-work path.
- Project explorer invites hover/focus but every project name is visible without interaction.
- No showreel gate or loader.

## 2. Proof stays one click away
Art direction must never increase the distance to evidence.

Design implications:
- Work rows are normal links.
- Resume and contact remain visible.
- Case-study routes and live-project links stay intact.

## 3. Editorial tension, functional controls
The layout may feel unusual; controls must remain conventional.

Design implications:
- asymmetry, overlap and mixed typography are allowed;
- links/buttons keep familiar cursor/focus behavior;
- no hidden navigation or gesture-only interaction.

## 4. One signature interaction, not many effects
The project-preview/index interaction is the main discovery device.

Design implications:
- other sections use lower motion intensity;
- no unrelated 3D, cursor trail, autoplay or infinite decorative loops.

## 5. Motion reveals structure
Motion communicates selection, continuity and hierarchy.

Design implications:
- preview changes use opacity/transform/clip-path;
- reveal animation remains one-shot;
- `prefers-reduced-motion` makes transitions immediate without removing content.

## Signature moments

1. **Arrival:** mixed DM Sans + Instrument Serif statement with layered portrait object.
2. **Project discovery:** sticky preview changes on hover/focus while numbered project index remains readable.
3. **Closing:** oversized editorial contact line with direct standard actions.

## Critique gate

A visual or motion choice stays only if it supports at least one of:
- recruiter orientation;
- project selection;
- proof visibility;
- brand/design authorship;
- interaction continuity.

Otherwise it is decoration and should be removed or reduced.
